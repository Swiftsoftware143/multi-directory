Clearinghouse Admin Guide

A practical walkthrough of the ZaarHub loyalty clearinghouse admin panel — how to configure it, what every view does, and exactly how money moves so you can set things up with confidence.

🪙

ZaarCash

1 ZaarCash = $0.01 (1¢) of purchasing value to the consumer. Points earn and redeem across every city in the network.

🏙️

One Wallet, Many Cities

A visitor earns in Palm Bay and redeems in St. Petersburg against the same member wallet and the same network treasury.

🎯

Category Caps

You control how much of an invoice can be paid with points per business category (e.g. 20% at grocery, 0% at tobacco).

⏳

Rolling Expiry

Points older than 365 days are expired automatically, protecting the treasury from stale liability.

🚀 First-Time Setup

The admin panel lives at https://directory.swiftsoftware.net/admin/. It uses the same sign-in as the rest of the Multi-Directory platform.

  1. Open the admin URL

    Go to https://directory.swiftsoftware.net/admin/ in your browser.

  2. Sign in

    Enter your Multi-Directory email and password. Any valid admin or operator account works — the clearinghouse routes are login-gated. (David's swiftsoftware143@yahoo.com super-admin account is a good default.)

  3. Pick a network

    Use the Network / City Directory dropdown in the top bar to select which network you're viewing. Options include ZaarHub (All Cities), Palm Bay, St. Petersburg, and more as cities come online. Each network has its own treasury and caps.

  4. Review the Treasury

    Start on the 📊 Treasury tab — confirm the live issued/redeemed numbers and economics for the network before changing anything.

  5. Set your category caps

    Go to 🎯 Category Caps and make sure each business category has the redemption limit you want. This is the single most important configuration for controlling exposure.

Signed out? The panel remembers your sign-in automatically (stored securely in your browser). If you get a 401 on any action, it will prompt you to sign in again.

💰 Economics & Billing — Read This First

This is the core of the clearinghouse. Every point creation and redemption has a real money side. The rules are fixed in the engine:

EventConsumer getsIssuing business billedRedeeming business reimbursedPlatform keeps
Issue (earn)1 point on a $1.00 purchase$0.01 / pt——
Redeem (spend)$0.01 off per point—$0.008 / pt—
Spread on every redemption———$0.002 / pt

Working example: a $25.00 purchase issues 25 points. The issuing business is billed $0.25. When that customer later redeems 10 points (worth $0.10 off), the redeeming business is reimbursed $0.08, and the platform keeps $0.02 as the spread (covers fees + breakage from never-redeemed points).

How the platform is protected: 1 point issued = $0.01 liability, but full redemption only pays $0.008. That $0.002/point margin is the platform's buffer — plus rolling 365-day expiry removes points that are never redeemed.
Billing is per-network and tracked per business. Use the Treasury and Business Ledgers views to see who owes what.

🎯 Category Caps

Controls the maximum percentage of an invoice that can be settled with ZaarCash, per business category. This is the lever that limits how much a single redemption can "discount" a sale.

  • 0% — no redemption allowed at that category (e.g. tobacco is typically 0%).
  • 100% — unlimited; an invoice could be fully paid with points.
  • Numbers in between limit the cap (e.g. 20% at grocery means at most $0.20 of every $1.00 can be points).

Canonical defaults (recommended):

CategoryDefault capRationale
grocery20%Low-margin — points capped at 20% of invoice
convenience20%Low-margin

tobacco0%Policy restriction
restaurant100%Full redemption allowed
retail100%Full redemption allowed
services100%Full redemption allowed
homegoods50%Mid-margin

To change a cap:

  1. Open Category Caps

    Select the 🎯 Category Caps tab for the network you're managing.

  2. Click Edit

    Next to the category row you want to change, click Edit.

  3. Set the percentage

    Enter a value between 0 and 100 and an optional description, then Save Cap. The change applies immediately and persists.

Tip: Put a descriptive note (like "Low-margin: points capped at 20% of invoice") so future admins understand why a cap is set.

🏪 Business Ledgers

A per-business monthly net position: how many points it issued, how many it redeemed, its total billed ($0.01/pt), total reimbursed ($0.008/pt), and the resulting Net Position (reimbursed − billed) for the month.

  • Positive net position — the business redeemed more than it issued this month; it's owed money.
  • Negative net position — the business issued more than it redeemed; it owes money (buy-in for the points it gave away).

This is where you reconcile individual businesses before settling with them.

📜 Clearing Logs

A chronological audit trail of every issue and redemption event in the network — who (member), which business, points, transaction amount, category, and timestamp. Use this for verification, disputes, and reconciliation. The view shows the most recent events (freshest first).

🛒 Scan Simulator

A built-in test utility that exercises the clearinghouse engine end-to-end. Use it to verify the economics ("bill $0.01/pt issued, reimburse $0.008/pt redeemed") without real traffic.

Issue (purchase / check-in): provide a visitor account UUID, business UUID + name, category, and transaction amount, then Issue ZaarCash. The engine awards points, bills the business, and logs the event.

Redeem (spend points): provide the visitor, business, category, invoice amount, and points to redeem, then Redeem ZaarCash. The engine enforces the category cap and the member's balance.

Gotcha: the Visitor Account UUID and Business UUID must reference rows that already exist in the database (visitor_accounts(id) and businesses(id)). Unregistered UUIDs return an error — this sim hits real production tables, so this is a testing tool, not a customer flow.

⏳ Expire Points

Runs a rolling 12-month expiry: any ZaarCash earned more than 365 days ago is removed from member balances and the network's outstanding liability. This is the same endpoint that a scheduled cron can call automatically.

  1. Open Expire Points

    Select the ⏳ Expire Points tab.

  2. Confirm the network

    The current network is shown. Expiry is per-network.

  3. Run Expiry Now

    Click the button. The result reports how many points were expired and how many members were affected.

Automation: a scheduled job can call this endpoint on a daily/weekly cadence so expiry runs on its own. Right now it's manual (or cron-driven once wiring is confirmed).

❓ FAQ

Q: How much does issuing a point cost the business?
A: $0.01 per point (1¢). A $25 purchase that yields 25 points bills the business $0.25.

Q: How much does a redemption reimburse the business?
A: $0.008 per point. Redeeming 10 points reimburses $0.08 and removes $0.10 of liability, netting the platform $0.02.

Q: Can a customer use points in a different city than where they earned them?
A: Yes. Members have one wallet per network, so they earn in Palm Bay and redeem in St. Petersburg against the same balance and treasury.

Q: What happens to points that are never redeemed?
A: They expire after 365 days via the rolling expiry, which protects the treasury from growing stale liability.

Q: Do caps apply per business or per network?
A: Per category, per network. You configure the cap for each category in the selected network, and it applies to every business in that category.

Q: My action returned 401.
A: Your sign-in expired. Sign in again and retry — the panel will re-prompt you.
